01 · Poisoning — dose response

Targeted label flips, class 2 → 7. Clean accuracy barely moves; the damage concentrates on the source class and shows up first in calibration (ECE).

Detection before degradation: the kNN label audit flags poisoned samples with recall — detection degrades as the poison fraction grows (poisoned samples stop looking anomalous). Audit-and-filter retraining recovers source-class accuracy to at a ~1pt clean-accuracy cost.

02 · Backdoor — patch trigger → class 0

3×3 patch stamped only on poisoned samples. Conventional validation sees a healthy model; the trigger works.

Cross-threat finding: the same data audit that cleans label-flip poisoning is ineffective against the backdoor — it recovers only of poison and ASR stays at . The model-level trigger-flip scan (flip-to-target rate on correct predictions) is the detector that works. Backdoor defense must inspect the model, not just the data.

03 · Extraction — substitute fidelity vs query budget

Target accuracy . Fidelity = substitute↔target agreement on held-out data.

Measured trade-off: output restriction (rounding, argmax-only) barely reduces fidelity ( drop) — on MNIST, hard labels alone suffice to steal the classifier. The rate limit is the only control that bites: it caps fidelity at regardless of the attacker's budget.

04 · Membership inference & privacy

Score-based MIA (confidence / loss / margin). Members = real training samples, non-members = test population.

Honest finding: on MNIST with 20k samples this CNN leaks almost nothing through its outputs (AUC ≈ 0.50 at every epoch count, and ≈ 0.51 even at 3k samples / 20 epochs). DP-style clipped+noisy training reliably suppresses the train–test gap ( → ) and pushes AUC below 0.5, at a measured accuracy cost of at the strongest setting.

05 · Adversarial examples — FGSM / PGD

Robustness–utility Pareto: adversarial training buys robustness at ε=0.1 for clean accuracy. Label smoothing costs nothing in clean accuracy and buys nothing — it is not a robustness defense.

06 · Supply-chain integrity

What integrity checking protects: the 1-ulp tampered artifact is behaviorally indistinguishable from the approved model ( prediction disagreement, accuracy unchanged) — behavioral testing cannot catch it. Hash + HMAC verification catches it before load, every time, with zero utility cost.

Methodology
Every figure on this page is measured from real training runs and real model queries (results/*.json in the repo; nothing hardcoded). Poisoning is targeted label-flip; MIA is score-based thresholding; the DP-style defense is batch gradient clipping + Gaussian noise — an approximation of DP-SGD without formal ε accounting.
